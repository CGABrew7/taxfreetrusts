Trusts and taxes

A revocable living trust changes who handles the assets. For deaths in 2026, the IRS basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000, and the trust does not remove its assets from that estate.

Income tax while you are alive

You report a revocable trust's income on your own return.

Section 676 treats the grantor as the owner when the grantor can revest title. The Instructions for Form 1041 say a revocable living trust is a grantor trust because it is revocable, and that Optional Method 1 lets the trustee give payers the grantor's name and taxpayer identification number instead of filing Form 1041. The Instructions for Form SS-4 say that trustee does not need an employer identification number for the trust while Optional Method 1 continues.

Federal estate tax

For deaths in 2026 the basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000, and a revocable trust does not take its assets out of the gross estate.

The IRS 2026 inflation adjustments state that basic exclusion amount for estates of decedents who die during 2026. The IRS estate tax page uses the same $15,000,000 as the 2026 filing threshold, and it says a surviving spouse's election to use a deceased spouse's unused exemption is made on a timely filed estate tax return. Section 2038 includes property you transferred if, at death, you still hold a power to alter, amend, revoke, or terminate who enjoys it. A life policy you own can be included under section 2042. An irrevocable life insurance trust is counsel work, not the $349 packet.

Basis at death

Property acquired from a decedent takes a basis of fair market value at the date of death, unless section 1014 names a different amount.

Section 1014(a) states that rule. An election under section 2032 uses the alternate valuation date in that section. Section 1014(b)(2) includes property transferred in trust when the decedent kept the right to revoke.
