TaxFreeTrusts

About

Hanok Ventures, operating TaxFreeTrusts. Fort Wayne, Indiana.

TaxFreeTrusts is a document-draft desk that sells a will or living trust packet, and explains how to fund it, for homeowners who want a house or a shop to pass to their kids.

You answer the questions, pay a posted price on Stripe, print a draft, and sign it with the witnesses or notary your state requires. The pages and drafts are education and documents. They are not legal advice.

What TaxFreeTrusts does

Will ($79)

The will is a $79 draft, paid on a Stripe Payment Link. It is the document for property still in your own name, a personal representative, and, when you have minor children, a guardian.

The outcome is a printable draft with those names filled in from the planner. It takes effect only after you sign it with the formalities your state requires. The draft is not legal advice, and it is not a court filing we make for you.

Living trust ($349)

The living trust is a $349 draft packet: a revocable living trust, a pour-over will, and a schedule of what you intend to move. While you are alive and competent, the draft says you may amend or revoke it. A successor trustee is named for the assets that were actually transferred in.

The outcome people buy it for is a house or a shop that can pass under the trust, for the assets you fund, without a probate file for those assets. A revocable living trust does not reduce federal income tax, and it does not replace a will for guardians. This is a draft, not legal advice.

Counsel desk ($790)

The counsel desk is $790, paid on Stripe. The support page states what that payment is: the attorney introduction. TaxFreeTrusts does not appear in court and does not give legal advice.

The outcome is an introduction path when the intake points past a simple packet, such as a large life policy or a business that may grow. Paying $790 does not, by itself, hire a lawyer or open a representation. The terms say the same thing.

Funding education

The funding afternoon is the step after the signature. Deed the home to yourself as trustee, update bank payable-on-death instructions, leave retirement accounts in your name, and stop before moving a large life policy. The living trust article names the failure mode: an unfunded house still titled in your individual name.

The outcome is a written list of transfers you complete yourself. The article is education. It is not legal advice, and the site does not record the deed or change the bank form for you.

What makes TaxFreeTrusts different

Posted prices, compared with a law-firm retainer

Traditional law-firm estate work is usually scoped as a retainer or a custom invoice. TaxFreeTrusts lists three prices on the site before you pay: $79 for a will draft, $349 for a living trust draft, and $790 for the counsel desk.

The posted price is what you pay for that draft or that introduction. A licensed attorney's work for the same family is a separate engagement, with its own scope. This page does not claim the draft equals that work, and it does not claim every law firm costs more.

A packet you print and sign yourself

The terms describe the product as a document you print and finish. Hanok Ventures does not file it with a court and does not take the matter as your law firm.

You read the draft, sign with your state's witnesses or notary, and, for a living trust, fund it. The terms of use say a draft in a drawer is not an executed will or trust.

The pay link follows the intake

On the start page, a large life policy or a business that may grow, combined with a net-worth band of $5 million or more, switches the pay link to the counsel desk. Other answers keep the living trust packet. The note on that screen still describes a living trust now, plus an ILIT review or a growth conversation for those larger cases.

The $790 payment is the introduction described on the support page. It is not a finished tax opinion inside the draft. Funding is written next to the draft

The living trust page and the funding page spell out the transfers, and the accounts to leave alone. Retirement accounts stay in your name. A large life policy is counsel work, which the tax article calls out as an ILIT conversation, not the $349 packet.

Those steps sit on the same site as the draft you print. You still complete the deed, the payable-on-death form, and any beneficiary change yourself.

How TaxFreeTrusts works

You start at the plan page. The homepage calls the intake the five questions. The page itself is five steps. Step one asks who the plan is for: legal name, state, marital status, spouse, and minor children. Step two asks what should move: a home, a home plus a shop or farm, guardianship, a life policy, or a business, plus who receives what remains and a backup. Step three asks who runs it: the successor trustee, and whether the family is straightforward, blended, or includes a child who should not receive a lump sum.

Step four is the recommendation. The pay link is the counsel desk when the asset is a large life policy or a business that may grow and the net-worth band is $5 million or more. Otherwise the pay link is the living trust. The homepage also offers the will on its own at $79. You pay on Stripe through a Payment Link. The card form is on Stripe.

Step five fills a draft in the browser. You print it and sign with the witnesses or notary your state requires. If the document is a living trust, you still fund it: deed, payable-on-death, retirement accounts left in your name. Support is email to plan@taxfreetrusts.com. A response time is not published. Frequently asked questions

Is a TaxFreeTrusts draft legal advice?

No. A draft is a document you print and finish. Reading the site, using the planner, or paying does not create an attorney-client relationship with Hanok Ventures or TaxFreeTrusts. Drafts are not legal advice and are not a substitute for a licensed attorney in your state.

How do I sign the draft?

Print the packet and sign it with the witnesses or notary your state requires. State formalities matter. A draft left unsigned is not an executed will or trust, and a living trust still has to be funded before the house is in it.

What is the refund policy?

Refunds are covered in the terms of use. Email plan@taxfreetrusts.com before we deliver the draft if you need to cancel. Once a draft has been delivered, the purchase is generally not refundable, except where the law requires a refund.

How do payments work?

Will ($79), living trust ($349), and counsel desk ($790) are charged on Stripe Payment Links. You enter the card on Stripe, and this site does not store full card numbers. Stripe may share the name, email, and amount needed to deliver the order.

Does the counsel desk hire a lawyer?

The support page calls the $790 desk the attorney introduction. TaxFreeTrusts does not appear in court and does not give legal advice. Paying $790 does not, by itself, hire a lawyer or open a representation.
