Living trust or probate

A revocable living trust holds assets you title in the trustee's name. You can serve as trustee while you are able. At death, the successor trustee handles those assets. Property you leave in your own name can still go through the probate court. A pour-over will sends leftover assets into the trust, and those leftover assets may pass through probate first.

The probate path

The Supreme Court of Georgia publishes Georgia Probate Court Standard Forms. The index includes a Petition for Letters of Administration, a Petition to Probate Will in Solemn Form, a Petition to Probate Will in Common Form, and a Petition for Discharge of Personal Representative. Those form titles are the ordinary probate path shown on that official page. The index does not set out the steps inside the forms.

Source: Georgia Probate Court Standard Forms, Supreme Court of Georgia

Small estate

The same Supreme Court of Georgia forms index lists GPCSF 9, Petition for Order Declaring No Administration Necessary. That is a petition, and the forms index page does not state an affidavit procedure or a dollar limit. There is no affidavit limit on that official page.

Source: Georgia Probate Court Standard Forms, Supreme Court of Georgia

Estate or inheritance tax

The Georgia Department of Revenue quotes O.C.G.A. section 48-12-1: on and after July 1, 2014, there shall be no estate taxes levied by the state and no estate tax returns shall be required by the state. The same page states that Georgia has no inheritance tax.

Source: Georgia Department of Revenue, Estate Tax FAQ
