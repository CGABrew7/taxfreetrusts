Living trust or probate

Arkansas

A will in Arkansas does not prove title until the circuit court admits it to probate. A living trust is a different instrument, and revocable means the settlor can revoke it without the consent of the trustee or a person holding an adverse interest. Revocable living trust or will

A will in Arkansas does not prove title until the circuit court admits it to probate. The 2022 benchbook prints section 28-73-103: revocable, as applied to a trust, means revocable by the settlor without the consent of the trustee or a person holding an adverse interest.

The Arkansas judiciary probate benchbook, updated 2022, prints Arkansas Code section 28-40-104: no will shall be effectual for the purpose of proving title to or the right to the possession of any real or personal property disposed of by the will until it has been admitted to probate. The same benchbook defines a settlor as a person, including a testator, who creates or contributes property to a trust. The trustee holds the assets titled to the trustee. A pour-over will can send leftover assets into the trust, and those leftover assets may pass through probate first.

Source: Arkansas judiciary probate benchbook, updated 2022

The probate path

In Arkansas, the estate is opened in the circuit court, probate division, in the county where the person lived.

The Arkansas judiciary probate benchbook, updated 2022, prints Arkansas Code section 28-40-102: venue for the probate of a will and for administration is the county where the decedent resided at death. If the decedent did not reside in this state, venue is the county where the greater part, in value, of the property in this state is situated. The same benchbook prints section 28-40-103: a will must be submitted within five years of the death, and administration must be granted within five years of the death. It prints section 28-40-104: no will shall be effectual for the purpose of proving title to or the right to the possession of any real or personal property disposed of by the will until it has been admitted to probate. Arkansas Judiciary Form 23 is the affidavit for collection of a small estate by a distributee, filed with the probate clerk of the circuit court. The form says distributees are entitled to the identified property without an order of the court, upon furnishing a clerk-certified copy of the affidavit.

Source: Arkansas judiciary probate benchbook, updated 2022

Small estate

Arkansas's small-estate figure on this page is $100,000 when the statute's conditions are met.

Form 23 states that the distributee affidavit may be used when no petition for a personal representative is pending or has been granted, more than forty-five days have elapsed since death, and the value, less encumbrances, of all property owned by the decedent at death, excluding the homestead of and statutory allowances for the benefit of the surviving spouse or minor children, if any, does not exceed one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000). The reporter's notes say that if the affidavit estate contains real property, the distributee may publish notice promptly after filing. The notes cite Arkansas Code section 28-41-101.

Source: Arkansas Judiciary Form 23, Affidavit for Collection of Small Estate by Distributee

Estate or inheritance tax

Arkansas has no estate tax for a death on or after January 1, 2005.

The Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration states that Act 645 of 2003 repealed Arkansas Estate Tax for estates of those who died on or after January 1, 2005, and that no estate tax return is required for decedents after December 31, 2004. That page does not mention an inheritance tax.

Source: Arkansas DFA Subject 605, Estate Tax, revised 12/22/2020

Questions

The answers use the same statute and court pages as the sections above.

Does a will prove title in Arkansas before it is probated?

No. The 2022 Arkansas judiciary probate benchbook prints Arkansas Code section 28-40-104: no will shall be effectual for the purpose of proving title to or the right to the possession of any real or personal property disposed of by the will until it has been admitted to probate. A living trust is a separate instrument. Assets titled to the trustee are handled by the successor trustee. Assets still in your name can be a probate matter.

What does revocable mean for a trust in Arkansas?

The 2022 probate benchbook prints Arkansas Code section 28-73-103. Revocable, as applied to a trust, means revocable by the settlor without the consent of the trustee or a person holding an adverse interest. A settlor is a person, including a testator, who creates or contributes property to a trust. The trust controls assets titled to the trustee.

How long do you have to submit a will in Arkansas?

The 2022 probate benchbook prints Arkansas Code section 28-40-103: a will must be submitted within five years of the death, and administration must be granted within five years of the death. Section 28-40-102 places venue in the county where the decedent resided at death.

Does a living trust avoid probate in Arkansas?

A revocable living trust holds the assets you title in the trustee's name. The successor trustee handles those assets. Property still titled in your own name can be a probate matter. A pour-over will can send leftover assets into the trust, and those leftover assets may still pass through probate first.

What is the small-estate procedure in Arkansas?

Form 23 states that the distributee affidavit may be used when no petition for a personal representative is pending or has been granted, more than forty-five days have elapsed since death, and the value, less encumbrances, of all property owned by the decedent at death, excluding the homestead of and statutory allowances for the benefit of the surviving spouse or minor children, if any, does not exceed one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000). The reporter's notes say that if the affidavit estate contains real property, the distributee may publish notice promptly after filing. The notes cite Arkansas Code section 28-41-101.

Does Arkansas tax an estate or an inheritance?

The Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration states that Act 645 of 2003 repealed Arkansas Estate Tax for estates of those who died on or after January 1, 2005, and that no estate tax return is required for decedents after December 31, 2004. That page does not mention an inheritance tax.
