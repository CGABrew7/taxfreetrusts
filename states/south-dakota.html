Living trust or probate

South Dakota

South Dakota does not set a price for a living trust, and the Department of Revenue says the state has no inheritance tax and no estate tax. SDCL 55-1-15 lets the trustor be the sole trustee and the sole beneficiary during the trustor's lifetime when the trust provides for a successor beneficiary at the trustor's death. Property left in your own name can still be a probate matter in circuit court.

Living trust cost and sole trustee

South Dakota does not set a price for creating a living trust. SDCL 55-1-15 says a trust which provides for one or more successor beneficiaries upon the death of the trustor is not invalid, merged, or terminated if the trustor is the sole trustee and the sole beneficiary during the trustor's lifetime.

SDCL 55-1-4 creates an express trust by words or acts that show an intention to create a trust and the subject, purpose, and beneficiary, and a trust that concerns real property must be evidenced in writing. Unless the terms of the trust expressly reserve a power to the settlor to revoke or modify it, SDCL 55-3-6 makes the trust irrevocable. The successor trustee handles assets titled to the trustee. Property left in your own name can still go through the circuit court.

Source: SDCL 55-1-15, Sole trustee and sole beneficiary

The probate path

In South Dakota, the estate is opened in the Circuit Court.

SDCL 16-6-9 gives the circuit court original jurisdiction in all matters of probate, guardianship, conservatorship, and settlement of estates of deceased persons. SDCL 29A-3-1201 lets a successor collect personal property by affidavit when the value of the entire estate, wherever located, less liens and encumbrances, does not exceed $100,000, thirty days have elapsed, no personal representative petition is pending or granted, and the decedent has not incurred Department of Social Services debt for nursing home or other medical institutional care.

Source: SDCL 16-6-9, Original civil jurisdiction of circuit court

Fees

In South Dakota, when a will does not set compensation, a personal representative may be allowed a statutory commission on personal property.

SDCL 29A-3-719 says personal representatives, attorneys, accountants, appraisers, and other agents are entitled to reasonable compensation for services. Reasonable compensation is based on the time and labor involved, the novelty and difficulty of the questions and the skill required, whether the work precludes other employment, the fee customarily charged in the locality for similar services, the nature and value of the estate assets, the income earned, and the responsibilities assumed, the time limitations, and the experience, reputation, diligence, and ability of the person. When compensation is not provided by will, or in an intestate proceeding, the personal representative may be allowed commissions on the amount of personal property accounted for, excluding personal property not ranked as assets: on the first one thousand dollars at the rate of five percent, on all sums in excess of one thousand dollars and not exceeding five thousand dollars at the rate of four percent, and on all sums in excess of five thousand dollars at the rate of two and one-half percent. On real property accounted for, compensation is just and reasonable and fixed by the court. Real estate sold by the personal representative in the probate proceeding is considered personal property.

Source: SDCL 29A-3-719, Compensation of personal representative

Small estate

South Dakota's small-estate figure on this page is $100,000 when the statute's conditions are met.

SDCL 29A-3-1201 says that thirty days after death, a person indebted to the decedent or holding tangible personal property or an instrument evidencing a debt, obligation, stock, or chose in action must pay or deliver it to a successor who presents an affidavit. The affidavit must state that the value of the entire estate, wherever located, less liens and encumbrances, does not exceed $100,000. Thirty days must have elapsed since death. No application or petition for appointment of a personal representative may be pending or granted in any jurisdiction. The decedent must not have incurred indebtedness to the Department of Social Services for medical assistance for nursing home or other medical institutional care. The claiming successor must be entitled to payment or delivery of the property.

Source: SDCL 29A-3-1201, Collection of personal property by affidavit

Estate or inheritance tax

South Dakota has no inheritance tax and no estate tax.

The South Dakota Department of Revenue states that South Dakota does not have an inheritance tax, that the voters repealed the state inheritance tax effective July 1, 2001, and that there is also no estate tax.

Source: South Dakota Department of Revenue, Taxes

Questions

The answers use the same statute and court pages as the sections above.

How much does a living trust cost in South Dakota?

South Dakota does not set a price for creating a living trust. The commission in SDCL 29A-3-719 is probate pay for a personal representative, not a price for the trust. When a will does not provide compensation, that commission may be five percent on the first $1,000 of personal property accounted for, four percent on the amount above $1,000 up to $5,000, and two and one-half percent above $5,000. Compensation on real property is fixed by the court.

Can I be the sole trustee of a South Dakota living trust?

Yes, when the trust provides for a successor beneficiary at the trustor's death. SDCL 55-1-15 says a trust which provides for one or more successor beneficiaries upon the death of the trustor is not invalid, merged, or terminated if the trustor is the sole trustee and the sole beneficiary during the trustor's lifetime.

Is a living trust revocable in South Dakota?

Only if the terms reserve that power. SDCL 55-3-6 says that unless the terms of a trust expressly reserve a power to the settlor to revoke or modify the trust, the trust is irrevocable. SDCL 55-1-4 creates an express trust by words or acts that show an intention to create a trust and the subject, purpose, and beneficiary. A trust that concerns real property must be evidenced in writing.

Does a living trust avoid probate in South Dakota?

A revocable living trust holds the assets you title in the trustee's name. The successor trustee handles those assets. Property still titled in your own name can be a probate matter. A pour-over will can send leftover assets into the trust, and those leftover assets may still pass through probate first.

What is the small-estate procedure in South Dakota?

SDCL 29A-3-1201 says that thirty days after death, a person indebted to the decedent or holding tangible personal property or an instrument evidencing a debt, obligation, stock, or chose in action must pay or deliver it to a successor who presents an affidavit. The affidavit must state that the value of the entire estate, wherever located, less liens and encumbrances, does not exceed $100,000. Thirty days must have elapsed since death. No application or petition for appointment of a personal representative may be pending or granted in any jurisdiction. The decedent must not have incurred indebtedness to the Department of Social Services for medical assistance for nursing home or other medical institutional care. The claiming successor must be entitled to payment or delivery of the property.

How are probate fees set in South Dakota?

SDCL 29A-3-719 says personal representatives, attorneys, accountants, appraisers, and other agents are entitled to reasonable compensation for services. Reasonable compensation is based on the time and labor involved, the novelty and difficulty of the questions and the skill required, whether the work precludes other employment, the fee customarily charged in the locality for similar services, the nature and value of the estate assets, the income earned, and the responsibilities assumed, the time limitations, and the experience, reputation, diligence, and ability of the person. When compensation is not provided by will, or in an intestate proceeding, the personal representative may be allowed commissions on the amount of personal property accounted for, excluding personal property not ranked as assets: on the first one thousand dollars at the rate of five percent, on all sums in excess of one thousand dollars and not exceeding five thousand dollars at the rate of four percent, and on all sums in excess of five thousand dollars at the rate of two and one-half percent. On real property accounted for, compensation is just and reasonable and fixed by the court. Real estate sold by the personal representative in the probate proceeding is considered personal property.

Does South Dakota tax an estate or an inheritance?

The South Dakota Department of Revenue states that South Dakota does not have an inheritance tax, that the voters repealed the state inheritance tax effective July 1, 2001, and that there is also no estate tax.
