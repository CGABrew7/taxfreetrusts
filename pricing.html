What makes the trust effective

Sign it with the witnesses or notary your state requires, then fund it. A draft left unsigned is not an executed trust.

Support says each state sets the number of witnesses and whether a notary is required. Nolo's Online Living Trust page says you sign the trust in front of a notary public and then transfer your property into the trust. Trust & Will's pricing page says to sign and notarize the documents upon receiving or downloading them. Terms on this site say the same limit in one line: a draft left unsigned is not an executed will or trust, and a living trust still has to be funded.

Funding is the transfer. A trust with nothing in it does not keep anything out of probate. For the house, sign a deed to yourself as trustee and record it in the county where the home sits. How to fund a living trust cites 12 USC 1701j-3(d)(8), which bars a lender from calling a due-on-sale clause on a home of fewer than five units when the transfer is into an inter vivos trust, the borrower is and remains a beneficiary, and the transfer does not move rights of occupancy. What a living trust does is the short version: you sign the trust, usually serve as your own trustee, and move assets in.

Questions before you pay

A living trust is the packet for a home you want kept out of probate. A will names a guardian. Counsel is the attorney introduction.

A revocable living trust or a will?

A will says who receives your property, but it goes through probate, the court process that settles an estate. A revocable living trust holds the assets you move into it during your life. When you die, your successor trustee hands those assets on without probate. You can amend or revoke the trust at any time. The living trust packet includes a pour-over will, so anything you never moved still has a destination.

What does funding the trust mean?

Funding is moving assets into the trust. For the home, that means signing and recording a new deed. For accounts, it means retitling them or adding a payable on death designation. A trust with nothing in it does not keep anything out of probate, which is why the packet comes with a funding list. Read how funding works.

When do I need the counsel desk?

The planner points to counsel when you report a large life policy or a business that may grow, and a net worth band of $5M or more. At that size, tax planning and business succession need a lawyer. The counsel desk introduces you to an attorney. Representation begins only if a lawyer accepts the matter.

Does my state levy an estate tax?

Illinois, New York, Massachusetts, and Washington levy a state estate tax on their state pages. Indiana, Ohio, Florida, and Texas show no current estate tax. Michigan is tied to a federal credit. Open the state page for the statute or revenue link. For deaths in 2026, the IRS basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000. A revocable trust does not remove assets from that estate.
