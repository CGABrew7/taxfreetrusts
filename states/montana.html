Living trust or probate

A revocable living trust holds assets you title in the trustee's name. You can serve as trustee while you are able.

At death, the successor trustee handles those assets. Property you leave in your own name can still go through the probate court. A pour-over will sends leftover assets into the trust, and those leftover assets may pass through probate first.

Fees

In Montana, probate compensation is reasonable compensation under the statute linked below.

Montana Code Annotated section 72-3-631 says a personal representative is entitled to reasonable compensation for services. If the will provides for compensation and there is no contract with the decedent regarding compensation, the personal representative may renounce the provision before qualifying and be entitled to compensation under that section. A written renunciation of fee may be filed with the court. Section 72-3-634 says that on a motion for settlement of fees the court must review and determine the reasonableness of compensation of any person employed by the personal representative, including any attorney, and the personal representative's own compensation.

Source: Montana Code Annotated section 72-3-631, Compensation of personal representative

Small estate

Montana's small-estate figure on this page is $100,000 when the statute's conditions are met.

Section 72-3-1101 says that 30 days after death, a person indebted to the decedent or holding tangible personal property or an instrument evidencing a debt, obligation, stock, or chose in action shall pay or deliver it to a claiming successor on an affidavit. The affidavit must state that the value of the probate estate, wherever located, less liens and encumbrances, does not exceed $100,000, except as provided in subsection (2); that 30 days have elapsed; that no application or petition for appointment of a personal representative is pending or has been granted in any jurisdiction; and that the claiming successor is entitled to payment or delivery. Subsection (2) lets the department of revenue refund unclaimed property of $5,000 or less regardless of the value of the estate.

Source: Montana Code Annotated section 72-3-1101, Collection of personal property by affidavit

Estate or inheritance tax

Montana has no current estate tax on the page linked below.

The Montana Department of Revenue says Montana no longer collects estate or inheritance taxes. It says the inheritance tax was repealed in November 2000 and does not apply to any death after January 1, 2001, and that Montana does not have an estate tax for deaths after 2004.

Source: Montana Department of Revenue, Montana Estate and Inheritance Tax
