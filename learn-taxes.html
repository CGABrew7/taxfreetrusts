Trusts and taxes

A revocable living trust is a probate tool. While you are alive, the IRS treats its assets as yours, and most families owe no federal estate tax at all.

Income tax while you are alive

A revocable trust is a grantor trust. You report its income on your own return, as before. When you serve as your own trustee, the trust usually does not file a separate return or need its own tax number.

Federal estate tax

For deaths in 2026, the IRS basic exclusion amount is $15 million per person. The IRS adjusts that amount for inflation in later years. A surviving spouse can use the unused part of the first spouse's exclusion by filing an estate tax return. The tax applies only to the amount over the exclusion.

A revocable trust does not change this math. Its assets count in your estate, just as they would without the trust. A large life policy you own can be included in that estate. An irrevocable life insurance trust is counsel work, not the $349 packet.

Basis at death

Because the assets count in your estate, your heirs generally receive a stepped-up basis. The value at your death becomes their starting point for capital gains.

State estate tax

StateState estate tax
Illinois, New York, Massachusetts, WashingtonYes
Indiana, Ohio, Michigan, TexasNone
FloridaNone. Homestead deeds are picky.

Other statesConfirm your state

Several other states and the District of Columbia also levy an estate tax, often with an exemption far below the federal one. A few states tax heirs instead, through an inheritance tax.

When tax planning matters

If your net worth is $5M or more and you carry a large life policy or a business that may grow, tax planning needs a lawyer. That is what the counsel desk is for.
