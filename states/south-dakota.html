Living trust or probate

A revocable living trust holds assets you title in the trustee's name. You can serve as trustee while you are able. At death, the successor trustee handles those assets. Property you leave in your own name can still go through the probate court. A pour-over will sends leftover assets into the trust, and those leftover assets may pass through probate first.

The probate path

SDCL 16-6-9 gives the circuit court original jurisdiction in all matters of probate, guardianship, conservatorship, and settlement of estates of deceased persons. SDCL 29A-3-719 entitles personal representatives and their attorneys and other agents to reasonable compensation, and, when a will does not provide compensation or the proceeding is intestate, allows stated commissions on personal property accounted for. The 2022 session law amending SDCL 29A-3-1201 allows collection of personal property by affidavit when the entire estate does not exceed $100,000.

Source: SDCL 16-6-9, Original civil jurisdiction of circuit court

Fees

SDCL 29A-3-719 states that personal representatives, attorneys, accountants, appraisers, and other agents are entitled to reasonable compensation for services. When compensation is not provided by will, or in an intestate proceeding, the personal representative may be allowed commissions upon the amount of personal property accounted for, excluding personal property not ranked as assets: on the first one thousand dollars at the rate of five percent; on all sums in excess of one thousand dollars and not exceeding five thousand dollars at the rate of four percent; on all sums in excess of five thousand dollars at the rate of two and one-half percent. Upon real property accounted for, compensation is just and reasonable and fixed by the court. Real estate sold by the personal representative in the probate proceeding is considered personal property.

Source: SDCL 29A-3-719, Compensation of personal representative

Small estate

2022 Session Law chapter 88, House Bill 1085, amended SDCL 29A-3-1201 so the affidavit must state that the value of the entire estate, wherever located, less liens and encumbrances, does not exceed $100,000. Thirty days must have elapsed since death. No application or petition for appointment of a personal representative may be pending or granted. The decedent must not have incurred indebtedness to the Department of Social Services for medical assistance for nursing home or other medical institutional care. Estate or inheritance tax

The South Dakota Department of Revenue states that South Dakota does not have an inheritance tax, that the voters repealed the state inheritance tax effective July 1, 2001, and that there is also no estate tax.

Source: South Dakota Department of Revenue, Taxes
