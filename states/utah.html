Living will or living trust

A living will and a living trust do different jobs. Utah Code section 75A-9-101, effective January 1, 2026, defines an advance health care directive as a power of attorney for health care, a health care instruction, or both.

That directive covers health care decisions in every Utah county, including Salt Lake County. It does not transfer the house. Utah Code section 75B-2-602, effective May 7, 2025, says that unless the terms expressly provide that the trust is irrevocable, the settlor may revoke or amend the trust. That rule does not apply to a trust created under an instrument executed before May 1, 2004. The successor trustee handles assets titled to the trustee. Property left in your own name can still go through the district court.

Source: Utah Code section 75A-9-101, Advance health care directive, effective January 1, 2026

The probate path

In Utah, the estate is opened in the district court of the county where the person lived.

The Utah Courts informal-probate page says a probate case may be filed in the district court of the county where the decedent lived at the time of death, or in the district court of the county where the decedent owned property, citing Utah Code 75-3-201. For a person who lived in Salt Lake County, that court is the district court of Salt Lake County. Informal probate asks the court to appoint a personal representative without a hearing and is usually used when all interested parties agree. Formal probate asks for appointment with a hearing, usually because there is disagreement. An informal probate case may not be filed until 120 hours have passed since the death, and the case must be filed within three years of the death. The applicant files an application, the filing fee, and a Utah District Court Cover Sheet for Probate Actions. If the court appoints the applicant, it signs letters of administration if there is no will or letters testamentary if there is a will.

Source: Utah Courts, Informal Probate

Fees

In Utah, a personal representative and an attorney are entitled to reasonable compensation. The statute does not set a percent of the estate.

Utah Code section 75-3-718 says a personal representative and an attorney are entitled to reasonable compensation for their services. If a petition seeks approval of that compensation and no interested person objects, reasonable compensation is the compensation sought in the petition. If an interested person objects, the court determines reasonable compensation for the personal representative based on the quality, quantity, and value of the services and the circumstances, including the practice for other fiduciaries in similar circumstances, and determines reasonable compensation for the attorney. The section does not set a percent of the estate. When a petition seeks approval of or objects to that compensation, the petitioner sends a copy to all interested persons at least 10 days before the hearing.

Source: Utah Code section 75-3-718, Compensation of personal representative and attorney

Small estate

Utah's small-estate figure on this page is $100,000 when the statute's conditions are met.

Utah Code section 75-3-1201, effective May 7, 2025, says that 30 days after death a holder of tangible personal property, including an instrument evidencing a debt, obligation, stock, or chose in action, shall pay or deliver it to a claiming successor on an affidavit. The affidavit must state that the value of the entire estate subject to administration, wherever located, less liens and encumbrances, does not exceed $100,000, that 30 days have elapsed, that no application or petition for appointment of a personal representative is pending or has been granted in any jurisdiction, and that the claiming successor is entitled to payment or delivery. For not more than four boats, motor vehicles, trailers, or semitrailers, the affidavit instead states that the value of the entire estate subject to administration, other than those vehicles, does not exceed $100,000. Shares of stock in a water company transferred under section 73-1-10 or Title 70A, Chapter 8, are not eligible for transfer under this part.

Source: Utah Code section 75-3-1201, Collection of personal property by affidavit, effective May 7, 2025

Estate or inheritance tax

Utah has no inheritance tax to file.

The Utah State Tax Commission says Utah's estate tax system was a pick-up tax based on the federal credit for state death taxes, that federal changes eliminated Utah's inheritance tax after December 31, 2004, and that there is no longer a basis for the Utah inheritance tax. It says Utah inheritance tax returns do not need to be filed and Utah does not require an Inheritance Tax Waiver. House Bill 77 of the 2026 General Session repeals Utah Code sections 59-11-101 through 59-11-107 and 59-11-109 through 59-11-115. The enrolled bill takes effect May 6, 2026, and those sections have retrospective operation for a taxable year beginning on or after January 1, 2026. The code chapter is titled Inheritance Tax Act (Repealed 5/6/2026).

Source: Utah State Tax Commission, Inheritance Tax

Does Utah have an inheritance tax?

No. The Utah State Tax Commission says inheritance tax returns do not need to be filed and Utah does not require an Inheritance Tax Waiver. Federal changes eliminated the tax after December 31, 2004. Is a living will the same as a living trust in Utah?

No. A living will is an advance health care directive. Utah Code section 75A-9-101, effective January 1, 2026, defines that directive as a power of attorney for health care, a health care instruction, or both. It covers health care decisions and does not transfer the house. A living trust holds property you title in the trustee's name. Utah Code section 75B-2-602, effective May 7, 2025, says that unless the terms expressly provide that the trust is irrevocable, the settlor may revoke or amend it. That rule does not apply to a trust created under an instrument executed before May 1, 2004. Utah Courts say a probate case is filed in the district court of the county where the person lived. For a Salt Lake County resident, that is the district court of Salt Lake County.

Do you file a Utah small estate affidavit with the court?

No. The Utah Courts informal-probate page says a small estate affidavit is not filed with the courts. The successor signs it before a notary and gives it to the person holding the property. That page says the affidavit may be used if the entire value of the estate is under $100,000, there is no real property, at least 30 days have passed, and no application for a personal representative has been filed. Utah Code section 75-3-1201, effective May 7, 2025, states the cap as the value of the entire estate subject to administration, wherever located, less liens and encumbrances, not exceeding $100,000.

Does a living trust avoid probate in Utah?

A revocable living trust holds the assets you title in the trustee's name. The successor trustee handles those assets. Property still titled in your own name can be a probate matter. A pour-over will can send leftover assets into the trust, and those leftover assets may still pass through probate first.

What is the small-estate procedure in Utah?

Utah Code section 75-3-1201, effective May 7, 2025, says that 30 days after death a holder of tangible personal property, including an instrument evidencing a debt, obligation, stock, or chose in action, shall pay or deliver it to a claiming successor on an affidavit. Shares of stock in a water company transferred under section 73-1-10 or Title 70A, Chapter 8, are not eligible for transfer under this part.

How are probate fees set in Utah?
